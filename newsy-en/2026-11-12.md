### S&P cuts Poland's rating outlook to negative; Pekao's outlook follows
S&P Global Ratings lowered Poland's outlook from stable to negative, kept the foreign-currency rating at A-/A-2 and cut the local-currency rating from A/A-1 to A-/A-2. The agency points to deteriorating public finances (general-government deficit forecast at 7.1% of GDP in 2026 and 2027, net debt above 70% of GDP in 2029) and political polarisation; it raised its 2026 GDP growth forecast to 3.6%. On 9 Nov S&P also changed Bank Pekao's outlook to negative, keeping A-/A-2, citing its concentration in domestic lending and a large portfolio of government bonds. The decisions were taken on 6 Nov (Poland) and 9 Nov (Pekao), i.e. before this issue's window, and were not covered in earlier issues. S&P actions on other Polish banks were not checked.

**Why it matters:** concerns PEO and country risk (zloty, Polish bond yields), which feed into bank valuations.
